# Orquesta Francisco Canaro

*"El Pirincho" / "El Zurdito" — The Most Recorded Tango Orchestra in History*

## Overview

Francisco Canaro led the most prolific recording career in tango history — over 3,700 documented recordings spanning six decades — and his milongas remain the most frequently played at milongas worldwide because of their relaxed tempo and clear rhythmic structure. A common Argentine saying held: "He's got more money than Canaro."

## The Leader

**Full Name:** Francisco Canaro (born Francisco Canarozzo)
**Born:** November 26, 1888 — San José de Mayo, Uruguay
**Died:** December 14, 1964 — Buenos Aires, Argentina
**Instrument:** Violin
**Nicknames:** "El Pirincho" (after a bird), "El Zurdito" (The Lefty)

### Biography

Canaro came from working-class immigrant origins and was largely self-taught on the violin. He is credited with introducing the double bass to tango orchestras, replacing the earlier flute that had characterized Guardia Vieja ensembles. This innovation gave tango its characteristic rhythmic foundation.

Beyond his musical talents, Canaro was tango's greatest entrepreneur and institution-builder. He was instrumental in founding **SADAIC** (Sociedad Argentina de Autores y Compositores de Música) in 1935, the Argentine authors' and composers' rights society that still protects musicians today.

He pioneered the **estribillista** (refrain singer) concept around 1924 (first recorded 1927), adding brief vocal choruses to instrumental arrangements — a format that would evolve into the full cantor de orquesta of the Golden Age.

Canaro introduced tango to Paris in 1925 and recorded prolifically for multiple labels throughout his career.

## Musical Style

- **Tempo:** Slow to medium — among the easiest for beginners
- **Feel:** Melodic, accessible, steady, uncomplicated
- **Best for:** Social dancing, milonga, vals
- **Character:** Warm, inviting, never challenging

Canaro's style prioritizes clarity and danceability over complexity. His arrangements are straightforward, making the rhythm easy to follow. His milongas in particular are beloved by DJs for their unambiguous beat and moderate tempo — perfect for warming up a dance floor or helping beginners find their footing.

### The Ada Falcón Partnership

The collaboration with singer **Ada Falcón** (1905–2002) produced some of tango's most romantic recordings. Their partnership ended mysteriously when Falcón abruptly retired from tango in 1942 and eventually became a nun, never publicly explaining her decision. The Canaro-Falcón recordings remain among the most beautiful in the tango canon.

## For DJs

- **Tempo range:** 54–62 BPM (slow to moderate)
- **Energy level:** Low to medium
- **Best placement:** Early in the night; beginner-friendly tandas; warming up the floor
- **Pairs well with:** Di Sarli (lyrical), Fresedo (elegant), early D'Arienzo
- **Avoid pairing with:** Pugliese (too different in energy), late Biagi (too syncopated)

### Tanda Building

Canaro works well as an opening tanda — the accessible rhythm gets dancers comfortable. His milongas are universally beloved and can anchor a milonga tanda at any point in the evening. His valses are romantic and straightforward.

## Institutional Contributions

Beyond his recordings, Canaro shaped tango as an industry:

- **SADAIC founding** (1935) — Musicians' rights society
- **Estribillista format** — Introduced vocal refrains to instrumental tango
- **Double bass introduction** — Changed tango's rhythmic foundation
- **Paris introduction** (1925) — Brought tango to European audiences
- **Recording industry** — Established models for orchestra recording contracts

## Legacy

Critics sometimes debate Canaro's artistic depth compared to the Big Four (D'Arienzo, Di Sarli, Troilo, Pugliese), but his contributions to tango as an institution are unmatched. His recordings remain the backbone of milonga playlists worldwide, and his slow tempos and clear rhythms make his music ideal for social dancing.

The approximately 3,792 documented recordings make him by far the most recorded tango musician in history — a testament to both his longevity and his commercial appeal.
