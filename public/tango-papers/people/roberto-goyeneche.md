# Roberto Goyeneche

*"El Polaco" — The Voice of Post-Golden Tango*

The voice of post-golden tango, Roberto Goyeneche — nicknamed "El Polaco" (The Pole) for his light complexion and light-colored eyes — embodied Buenos Aires bohemian life and brought profound poetic intensity to tango singing through a raspy, emotional style that prioritized feeling over technique.

## Biography

### Early Life

Roberto Goyeneche was born on January 29, 1926, in Buenos Aires. His nickname "El Polaco" came from his light complexion and eyes, suggesting Polish ancestry, though his family was Argentine.

### The Bus Driver Who Became a Legend

Before becoming a professional tango singer, Goyeneche worked as a bus driver on Buenos Aires city lines. He began singing in amateur competitions and small venues, initially imitating Carlos Gardel's style before developing his own distinctive voice.

### With Aníbal Troilo

Goyeneche joined Aníbal Troilo's orchestra in 1956, beginning one of tango's most important singer-bandleader partnerships. With Troilo, he recorded some of the era's most emotionally powerful tangos, including "La Última Curda" (1956, lyrics by Cátulo Castillo) — considered by many to be the last great tango.

### With Horacio Salgán

He also collaborated extensively with pianist and orchestra leader Horacio Salgán, bringing his raspy, emotionally intense style to Salgán's sophisticated arrangements.

### The Bohemian Life

Goyeneche epitomized the 1950s Buenos Aires archetype — a regular at the cafés of Corrientes Avenue, notorious for late nights, drinking, and the bohemian lifestyle that fed his artistic intensity but damaged his health. This lifestyle gave his voice its characteristic raspy quality in later years.

### Late Career Masterworks

Paradoxically, it was in his later years, when his voice had been ravaged by decades of smoking and drinking, that Goyeneche achieved his most profound interpretations. The weathered quality of his voice added emotional depth that listeners found devastatingly authentic. His late recordings are considered masterworks of the genre.

## Style & Philosophy

Goyeneche prioritized emotional truth over technical perfection. Where other singers aimed for beauty of tone, he aimed for authenticity of feeling. His rubato — the elastic stretching and compressing of time — became legendary, with phrases that seemed to hang suspended before resolving.

His late recordings, with a voice damaged by years of hard living, achieved a raw poetic intensity that many consider the purest expression of tango sentiment. He didn't sing about loss and longing — he embodied them.

## Legacy

Goyeneche represents the link between the Golden Age singers and contemporary tango. His willingness to sacrifice vocal beauty for emotional truth influenced generations of singers who followed. The phrase "singing like Goyeneche" has become shorthand for prioritizing feeling over technique.
